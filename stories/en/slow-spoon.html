The Slow Spoon

Why the stomach's letter to the brain always seems to arrive twenty minutes late.

Level A1 · Beginner

Amira ate lunch fast. Seven minutes, every day, at her desk. By three o'clock, she was hungry again.

Her uncle had a small restaurant. He watched her eat once. He laughed. "Your stomach is fast," he said. "But your brain is slow."

"Your stomach sends a letter to your brain," he said. "The letter says: I am full. But the letter takes twenty minutes to arrive."

"You finish your food before the letter comes," he said. "So your brain does not know yet. You eat fast, so you eat more."

The next day, Amira ate slowly. She put her spoon down between bites. Lunch took longer. But the afternoon hunger did not come. She waited for the letter, and this time, it arrived.
