The Forest That Stopped Shrinking

Every August, satellites add up the forest the Amazon has lost, and in 2026 the number was the smallest in thirteen years.

Level A1 · Beginner

High above Brazil, a satellite flies over the Amazon forest. It takes pictures every day. Computers look for green that turns brown. Brown means lost trees.

Brazil's space agency runs this system. Every brown patch is an alert. Every year, the alerts are added up.

In August 2026, the scientists added up the alerts again. They found the smallest number in thirteen years. It was over a third less than last year. It was less than half of the ten-year average.

The Amazon is the biggest rainforest on Earth. It is home to many animals and plants. Brazil wants zero lost forest by 2030.

You cannot see this number from your window. But it is good news. This year, more green stayed green.
