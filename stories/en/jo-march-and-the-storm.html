What Jo March Learned About Storms

A line from Louisa May Alcott's Little Women, and what it really means to feel ready.

Level A1 · Beginner

Louisa May Alcott wrote a famous book. It is called "Little Women." It was published in 1868. In the book, four sisters grow up during hard times. They learn to be brave. One sister is named Jo March. She has a strong, independent spirit.

One day, Jo has a hard moment. She says something famous: "I am not afraid of storms, for I am learning how to sail my ship."

Jo does not mean a real storm. She means the hard parts of life. Disappointment. Loss. Not knowing what will happen. Everyone must go through these things. Jo does not say the storms will stop. She says she is learning to handle them.

This is the real meaning: Jo is not saying she has no fear. She is saying she gets better with practice. Every hard day teaches her something new.

More than 150 years later, people still say Jo's words. They remember them in hard moments. You do not need to feel ready first. You can learn while things are already difficult.
